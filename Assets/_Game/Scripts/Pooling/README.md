# Pooling sample

The Week 5 Lab A patterns on one scene. `SpawnPool.cs` creates every item in `Awake` (prewarm), hands them out with `Spawn` and takes them back with `Release`, so play never calls `Instantiate` or `Destroy`; when it runs dry it creates one more and warns once, which means `size` is too small. `Pooled.cs` is the thing it spawns (your enemy, chunk, tile or particle): it resets in `OnEnable` because it is reused, not new, and returns itself when its lifetime ends. `WaveTimer.cs` is the coroutine conversion: `await Awaitable.WaitForSecondsAsync(interval, ct)` with a token linked to `Application.exitCancellationToken`, and `OperationCanceledException` caught as the normal way out. Copy those three. The tests are in `Assets/_Game/Editor/Tests/SpawnPoolTests.cs`.

`TapToRelease.cs` stands in for your core verb (a tap returns an item to its pool) and `PoolingDemoHud.cs` is demo scaffolding: watch `created` stay at 32 at the default rate, then press Faster three times and watch it climb. On the phone, a Development Build with the Profiler attached should show no `Instantiate` after load and 0 B GC Alloc on frames without a spawn. A spawn frame shows a few dozen bytes under `Awaitable.RunContinuation`: that is the cancellation token being registered for the next wait, the price of a loop that stops the moment it is cancelled, not your spawn code. Design notes are in the `MGD_MODULE_GDD` vault, note `Samples/Pooling`.
