# Tiers sample

The Week 9 Lab A device tiers. `DeviceTier.cs` in `Scripts/Shared/` picks a tier once, before the first scene loads: High for 8 GB of RAM and 8 cores or more, Mid for 4 GB and 6 cores, Low below that, unless the `tier_override` key in PlayerPrefs names one. It sets the matching quality level (Low, Mid, High: index 0, 1, 2), each with its own URP asset in `Assets/_Game/Settings/Quality/` (render scale 0.75 / 0.9 / 1.0, MSAA off / 2x / 4x, textures at half / full / full resolution), logs `tier=<tier> detected=<tier> override=<bool> ram=<n>MB cores=<n>`, and only then applies the player's performance mode. `SystemInfo.systemMemorySize` reports the RAM Android gives apps, a little below the size on the box, so a phone sold as 8 GB can fall below the deck's 8192 MB line and land on Mid: the Pixel 7a reports `ram=7466MB` and picks Mid (the `tier=` line's `ram=` value shows what yours reports), where High FPS is greyed out; force High from the Tiers scene to try High FPS on such a phone. The lab sheet orders the two with Script Execution Order (DeviceTier at -100, PerformanceMode at -50); here both are static, so `DeviceTier` simply calls `PerformanceMode.ApplySaved()` after setting the tier. Balanced runs at the tier's own cap (Low 30 fps, Mid and High 60) and scale; High FPS (120 at 0.8) takes effect only on the High tier, and below it the pause card's toggle is greyed out as *High FPS (High tier only)* while the saved choice is kept. The tier also goes into `session_start` as `tier`. It differs from the deck's version in four ways: it is static rather than a component on the boot object; it ignores a saved override that is not a tier (the deck trusts any number); it checks the quality levels are Low, Mid, High before switching; and its log line also names the detected tier, the override, RAM and cores. Copy `DeviceTier.cs`, the changes to `PerformanceMode.cs`, and `Editor/Tiers/AssetBudgetRules.cs` with the audit menu. The tests are `DeviceTierTests`, `PerformanceModeTests`, `TierReadoutTests` and `AssetBudgetRulesTests` in `Assets/_Game/Editor/Tests/`.

`TierReadout.cs` is this scene: what was picked and why, what the quality level changes, and the override dropdown (Auto, Low, Mid, High) that saves `tier_override` and applies from the next launch. A thin opaque bar turning on a full-rect quad shows MSAA on its edges (MSAA smooths geometry edges only, so a soft-edged sprite would show nothing); a checker texture with mipmaps shows the Low tier's half-resolution textures. Two menu items go with it: *MGD Samples > Set Up Quality Tiers* (`Editor/Tiers/QualityTiersSetup.cs`) creates the three levels and URP assets, and *MGD Samples > Audit Asset Budgets* (`Editor/Tiers/AssetBudgetAudit.cs`) lists every texture and audio clip under `Assets/_Game` that breaks the Part C import rules; the budgets and how to measure them are in `docs/asset-budgets.md`. The lab table's Main Light Shadows row is a Universal (3D) Renderer setting and does nothing under the 2D Renderer this project uses. Post-processing and 2D shadows (Light2D with ShadowCaster2D) do exist in 2D, but no sample uses them, so the levels leave them out; a 2D game that uses them tiers the camera's post-processing and the lights' shadows instead. Design notes are in the `MGD_MODULE_GDD` vault, note `Samples/Tiers`.
